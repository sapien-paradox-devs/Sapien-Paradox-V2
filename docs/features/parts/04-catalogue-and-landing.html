Why it exists

D10 said readers would be created by concierge onboarding, so / was going to redirect to /login and a stranger would see nothing. D47 reversed that: payments lead onboarding, so the product needs a front door that explains itself. The landing page is that door, and it leads with the idea of reading this way rather than with a form.

Everything reachable without a session lives behind one filter — is_published — so a half-built book with three of its eight chapters uploaded is not for sale, and its cover cannot be fetched by guessing the slug.

What a stranger can touch

What each part does

01
core/api/checkout.py · GET /api/books
What is for sale: slug, title, price in minor units, chapter count, ordered by title, is_published only. It lives in the checkout router because buying is the only thing the list is for.
02
core/api/books.py
A book's public media. /cover streams the image through the API with an hour of cache and nosniff; /sample returns a short-lived signed URL for the sample video. Both resolve the book through one helper that filters on is_published.
03
core/services/media.py
Where the bytes and the URLs actually come from — the only module that knows how a storage URL is made, so the bucket stays swappable. Covered in feature 19.
04
pages/landing/index.tsx
Composes the five sections, holds the selected pace in useState, and navigates to /begin?pace=…. Deliberately has no machine: the convention requires one for async work, waiting or retrying, and this page does none.
05
pages/landing/Hero.tsx · Idea.tsx
The idea before the product: no form in the hero, then the three principles as a numbered list — numbered because they are read in order, not for decoration.
06
pages/landing/HowItWorks.tsx
The four steps, plus the strip that shows when the first five chapters would arrive at each pace. Real dates, never a countdown (D11).
07
pages/landing/CompanionSample.tsx · Faq.tsx
A short transcript showing the companion's tone — showing it does more than describing it — and the questions a stranger has before paying, as native <details> with no JavaScript.
08
lib/labels.ts · landing section
Every word on the page, including the FAQ pairs and the sample transcript. The longest section in the file, and the one most likely to be edited by someone who is not editing code.

Gotchas

  • The published filter has to be on the media endpoints too. Filtering only the list would leave an unfinished book's cover and sample reachable by guessing its slug — which is exactly the thing is_published was added to prevent.
  • "Public sample" does not mean a public bucket. Anyone may ask for the URL; the URL itself expires. The bucket stays private, and nothing in the codebase ever hands out a permanent storage path.
  • The pace intervals are declared twice. The backend owns the real schedule and it is env-overridable, so a test deployment can shrink it; the landing strip needs numbers at build time and carries its own PACE_INTERVAL_DAYS for display. Two places that can drift — the fix is moving the value into shared/constants.json.
  • A cover gets a new storage name on every upload. That is what makes an hour of browser cache safe: a corrected cover is a different URL, so nobody is served the old one from cache.
  • The landing page does the pace selection, the begin page does the buying. They were one page, and asking for money before explaining anything is what that page was replaced for.
