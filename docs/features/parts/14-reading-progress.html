Why it exists

D11 said no progress bars: no timers, no metadata, nothing that turns reading into a task list. D70 reversed half of that, because a reader coming back to a chapter after three days genuinely wants to know where they were — and the reversal is written down rather than smuggled in. What it permits is deliberately small: a ring per chapter, a hairline as you read, and a check when you say you are done.

The constraint around it matters more than the feature. Nothing in access, grants, delivery or cadence may read this table. Chapters unlock on a schedule, never on completion, and a test holds that line — because coupling unlocks to reading would quietly turn the product into a course.

From a scroll position to a row

What each part does

01
components/PdfChamber/position.ts
Two pure functions, testable with no DOM. readFraction is zero until the pages reach the top of the screen and one when their last line reaches the bottom — counting everything merely visible as read would open a short chapter already half full. A chapter that fits on one screen is read at a glance: 1.
02
components/PdfChamber/useReadingPosition.ts
Reads the scroll position at most once per frame and reports the fraction and the page being read. It measures the DOM and hands the result over; it holds no policy.
03
pages/reader/machine/ · the progress region
idle → waiting (a debounce, so scrolling does not post per frame) → sending, with guards movedEnough, isFurther, moreToSend. A failed save returns to idle because the next movement carries the same number or a larger one — there is nothing to retry.
04
lib/fetcher.ts · send()
The last word of a reading session: a keepalive POST fired on pagehide, with errors dropped because there is nobody left to show them to.
05
core/api/grants.py · progress · complete
Two endpoints, both 204, both grant-authenticated, both dispatching to the reading machine rather than calling the service directly.
06
core/machines/reading/ · the six internal rows
RECORD_PROGRESS and COMPLETE each get three rows — allowed, expired, not owner — and all of them have dest: None, because reading further changes nothing about the grant's state.
07
core/services/progress.py
Two idempotent writes under select_for_update. record clamps to 0.99 and only ever moves forward — rereading a paragraph is not losing ground. complete stamps the timestamp and sets 1.0, and asking twice changes nothing.
08
core/selectors.py · progress_for · progress_by_chapter
The reads. Home and the profile page use the batch version — one query for a whole shelf — and a NONE default so a chapter with no row needs no branch at the call site.
09
components/ChapterList/ · the quiet part
A small ring per chapter and a check with "Completed" once the reader marks it. No percentages on the rows, no badges, nothing that turns a shelf into a dashboard.

Gotchas

  • Progress is keyed on the reader and the chapter, not on the grant. A re-issued link is a new grant, and the reader's place has to survive it — which also means a reader holding only a token can write progress, because the row is reached through grant.user.
  • Reaching the last page is not finishing. record stops at 0.99 and only complete reaches 1, so completion is always something the reader declared.
  • Nothing in cadence may read this table. The temptation is real — "unlock the next chapter when they finish this one" is a plausible product — and it is the opposite of the premise. The rule is enforced by a test, not by memory.
  • The progress region must be parallel to the chamber. One sequence of states would have to leave reading to represent saving, which means the page changes under a reader because a background POST is in flight.
  • A dropped save needs no retry. The next scroll event carries a number at least as large, so retry logic here would add failure modes to buy nothing.
  • The furthest point is monotonic in the service, not in the client. Two devices, a stale tab or a replayed keepalive request all send whatever they last knew, and the database is where "only forward" is actually enforced.
  • Resuming where you stopped is not built. The number is stored and shown; the chamber does not yet scroll to it. That is the obvious next leaf, and it is listed as one.
