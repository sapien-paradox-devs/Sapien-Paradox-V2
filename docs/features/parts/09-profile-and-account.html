Why it exists

Until this landed, a signed-in reader had no page about themselves: no way to see what they owned, correct a misspelled name, change a password they had set by link, or find out how far through a book they were. The profile page is also where the header's avatar leads, which is the first thing a reader looks for when they want to know whether they are signed in as the right person.

It is the one feature here with no machine on the server side. Profile edits are CRUD, not a flow with states — nothing waits, nothing retries, and no guard decides anything a validator could not.

Where each field comes from

What each part does

01
core/api/profile.py · _profile()
Builds the whole payload: the account fields, hasPassword from has_usable_password(), and one BookProgressOut per order with chapters touched, chapters total and mean progress.
02
core/selectors.py · progress_by_chapter
The reader's places, keyed by chapter id, with a NONE default so a chapter with no row needs no branch at the call site. Reads live here rather than in the API layer.
03
core/api/profile.py · PATCH /api/profile
Writes avatar_seed directly, and routes name and email through onboarding.update_reader, translating OnboardingRefused into a 409 carrying the reason.
04
core/services/onboarding.py · update_reader
Validates the email is free, applies the change, and leaves an AccountChange row with by set to whoever made it — the reader themselves, or a staff member.
05
core/api/profile.py · POST /auth/password
Eight characters minimum; the current password required only from a reader who has one; then update_session_auth_hash, without which changing a password signs you out of the session you just used.
06
core/schemas/profile.py
Four schemas, all fields optional on the way in so a PATCH can carry one change. ProfileUpdateIn has no phone — see the gap below.
07
pages/profile/machine/machine.ts
Three parallel regions (D42): data loads, saving handles the details form, password handles the password form. Independent, so a failed password change does not blank the page or discard an unsaved name.
08
lib/avatar.ts
A deterministic identicon: a 5×5 symmetric grid coloured from the Jewel palette, hashed from the seed. A pure function — no network, no third-party avatar service, and no tracking pixel on every page.
09
components/Avatar/ · components/SiteHeader/
The avatar renders the identicon and falls back to the email as its seed. In the header it opens the dropdown that leads to the profile, the admin (for staff) and sign-out.

The gap that bites on the first real reader

Changing a phone number

A reader cannot change their own phone number: ProfileUpdateIn has no such field. The number is both the delivery address and half the identity, so a reader who switches numbers becomes permanently unreachable — every future chapter goes to a handset they no longer own, and password recovery goes with it. Staff can change it through the admin, which makes this a support request rather than a self-service action, but it is the account feature most likely to be needed first.

Also absent, and recorded rather than forgotten: changing an email is possible while deleting an account is not — D80's deactivate-and-erase pair exists on the staff side only.

Gotchas

  • Forget update_session_auth_hash and changing a password logs the reader out. Django invalidates sessions tied to the old password hash, so the reader is bounced to sign-in the moment they succeed.
  • A reader with no usable password must be able to set one without the current one. They arrived by link and have nothing to type — requiring it would lock exactly the readers this form exists for out of it.
  • chaptersUnlocked counts chapters the reader has actually opened, not chapters cadence has released. It is a progress number, and naming it "unlocked" is a trap worth knowing about when reading the payload.
  • The avatar seed defaults to the email. So a reader who never touches it still gets a stable icon, and changing the email changes the icon — which is why the column exists at all: it lets the icon be pinned.
  • The per-book loop queries per order. Fine at one or two books per reader, and worth collapsing into one query the moment a shelf gets long.
  • The profile tests broke the deploy, not the tests. Actor stubs without input types passed locally and failed the Vercel build's stricter type check — a reminder that "green locally" and "green on the host" are different claims.
