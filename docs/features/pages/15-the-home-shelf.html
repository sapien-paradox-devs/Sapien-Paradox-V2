The Home Shelf

The reader's own library: no tokens in the payload, dates instead of countdowns, and a link that mints on demand.

ReadingD11D42D70D79built

Why it exists

Home is the screen a reader returns to most, and it answers one question: what do I own, and where am I in it. Its two rules are both about restraint. No tokens in the payload — a link is minted when the reader asks for one, because five credentials in a response that gets cached, logged and screenshotted would undo expiry entirely. And books come from Order, never from "all published books", so nobody is shown a library they have not bought.

Three queries, whatever the shelf

What each part does

01
core/api/home.py
Three queries — orders with their books, the ids of every chapter this reader has ever opened, and every progress row — then one BookOut per order with a ChapterOut per chapter. Ordered by when the reader bought each book.
02
core/selectors.py · progress_by_chapter
Every chapter this reader has progress on, keyed by chapter id, in one query. Reads live here so the API layer does not query the ORM to make decisions.
03
core/schemas/home.py
The typed payload: HomeOut → BookOut → ChapterOut. Every field the shelf renders, and no field it does not.
04
pages/home/machine/machine.ts · the list region
loading → ready | empty | error. empty is its own state and says so plainly, so a blank frame does not read as a failure.
05
pages/home/machine/machine.ts · the send region
idle → sending → sent | limited | failed, in parallel with the list. A rate-limited send is a normal state, not an error: the reader is told the chapter is already on its way.
06
components/ChapterList/
The rows: number, title, a read mark, the progress ring, a check when completed, and the send-to-WhatsApp action. One component, so a book's chapters look the same wherever they appear.
07
pages/home/HomeSkeleton.tsx
The shape of the shelf while it loads — rows at the right height, so the page does not jump when the data lands.
08
core/api/read.py · the two session endpoints
Where a Home tap goes: GET /api/read/{id} mints or reuses a grant and the page redirects to the chamber; POST /chapters/{id}/send sends the link to the reader's own WhatsApp, reusing the live grant so two presses do not leave two live links.

What Home does not show yet

The reader-facing half of cadence

Chapters are minted scheduled with an unlock_at on each, and Home renders none of it: a reader on a weekly pace sees the chapters they can open and no sign of the rest. The payload has no locked rows and no dates, so a schedule they are living inside is invisible to them. Because every unlock moment is computed at purchase, Home can render the whole schedule without simulating anything — which is exactly why D50 chose to compute them up front.

Also waiting: grouping a shelf of several books properly, the cover grid that would share one BookCard with the public catalogue, and whatever a finished reader should see when the last chapter has been delivered.

Gotchas

  • Books come from orders, not from published books. The obvious query shows every reader the whole catalogue as though they owned it.
  • The read mark is first-open, not last-open. opened_at is an engagement signal rather than analytics, and re-opening inside the seven days must not restamp it.
  • The two regions have to be parallel. With one sequence, sending would have to leave ready — blanking the chapter list for one button press.
  • An empty context inside as const freezes. books: [] declared inline becomes readonly [], which the machine's context type rejects; the initial context is hoisted and annotated instead. The as const itself is needed so type: "parallel" stays a literal.
  • 429 is not an error here. "Already sent — check WhatsApp" is the truth, and rendering it as a failure would make a reader press again.
  • The per-book loop runs a query per book. Two queries cover progress and opens for the whole shelf, but chapters are fetched per order — fine at one or two books, worth collapsing when a shelf grows.
