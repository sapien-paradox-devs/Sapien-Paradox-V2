The Book Workspace

Drop a folder of PDFs and watch them become chapters: signed uploads straight to R2, auto-matched, fixed by drag.

StewardshipD78D82D84D85D86built

Why it exists

Uploading a book used to mean Django admin: one chapter at a time, a file field per form, no validation, and no way to see whether the thing was ready. D78 proposed uploading a book as a folder; D84 turned that into a draft workspace rather than a wizard — the book exists from the moment you name it, and you fill it in whatever order you like.

The workspace protects readers with two rules. A book is created unpublished and can only be published when its checklist passes. Once published, chapter order is fixed and a chapter anyone holds a link to cannot be deleted — because grants, progress and cadence all point at chapters by position.

Dropped folder to published book

What each part does

01
pages/admin/book/files.ts
A dropped folder arrives as a directory entry rather than as files, so this walks it and hands the flat list to the machine as one event. Browser APIs only, no network.
02
pages/admin/books/match.ts
Pure matching, tested without a DOM. PDFs are ordered by the number in the filename when there is one, otherwise by natural name order — so Chapter 2 precedes Chapter 10 — and titles are cleaned from the filenames. Videos match a chapter by number then by title; sample.mp4 is the public sample, and anything ambiguous waits in a tray. A match only proposes.
03
pages/admin/book/machine/ · the three regions
data loads the book, ops runs one change at a time, uploads is the queue. Staged files live in context until CONFIRM, so titles, order and video slots can all be fixed before anything is sent.
04
pages/admin/book/machine/ · the upload queue
Each file is an item, and while it moves it is a spawned child actor named by its id. PDFs go strictly one at a time and in order, so chapters land in the sequence the admin confirmed; everything else runs two at a time. Every finished upload returns the whole book, so the screen redraws from the server's truth rather than guessing at it.
05
core/services/uploads.py · start
Validates the destination and the file's kind — PDF, MP4 or image — and its size against the per-kind cap, chooses a fresh storage key in the same shape as the models' upload paths, and returns a signed ticket plus how to send the bytes (one PUT, or one per part above the multipart threshold).
06
core/services/uploads.py · direct · complete
direct is the no-R2 fallback so a fresh clone gets the same screens; complete verifies the object exists at the promised size and returns the ticket's claims for attaching.
07
core/services/catalog.py · create_book · update_book
A new book is unpublished from the start, with a unique slug derived from the title. Editing details never changes the slug — it is already in links that have been shared.
08
core/services/catalog.py · checklist · publish · unpublish
Three booleans and a passes property. Unpublishing takes a book off sale and does not touch anyone's reading: access comes from Order, not from this flag.
09
core/services/catalog.py · reorder · delete_chapter · attach · prepare
Reordering and deletion are draft-only, and deletion is refused outright for a chapter anyone holds a grant to. attach puts an uploaded object where its ticket said it goes; prepare extracts the text and renders the pages, and reports whether the chapter is ready.
10
core/api/admin_books.py
Fifteen staff endpoints that map HTTP to those calls and nothing else: books, chapters, media removal, cover bytes, ordering, a render retry, and the three upload steps.
11
pages/admin/book/ · the panels
Details, Chapters, DropZone, Uploads, Videos, Publish — one workspace, each panel a view onto the same machine rather than a step in a wizard.

Gotchas

  • Chapter order is identity. Grants, progress and the cadence schedule all point at a chapter by position, so reordering a published book would quietly hand readers a different chapter than the one they were sent. Hence: draft only.
  • PDFs must upload one at a time. Chapters are appended in arrival order, so concurrent uploads would scramble the sequence the admin just confirmed — which is why the queue treats PDFs differently from videos and covers.
  • Nothing uploads until CONFIRM. Auto-matching gets filenames wrong often enough that fixing them after a batch upload would mean renaming chapters in the database instead of dragging rows on screen.
  • A signed ticket, not a pending-upload table. No row to create, expire or clean up, and nothing to leak — but it is still kept out of logs.
  • The upload path must survive having no R2. Otherwise the whole workspace is unusable on a fresh clone, and mandate 6 stops being true.
  • A render failure must not fail the upload. prepare reports readiness rather than raising, the checklist blocks publishing until every chapter is rendered, and there is a retry action for the one bad file.
  • Completion has to verify the bytes. A browser that failed mid-PUT would otherwise attach a truncated object, and the first reader to open that chapter would be the one to find out.
